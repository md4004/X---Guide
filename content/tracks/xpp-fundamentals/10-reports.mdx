<Step id="two-halves">


An F&O report is not one thing. It is:

1. a **report data provider** — an X++ class that runs on the server, queries whatever it
   needs, and writes rows into a table, and
2. a **design** — which reads that table and prints it, with grouping, totals and page
   breaks.

They never talk directly. The provider's only output is the table; the design's only input
is the table. That is the entire contract, and it is why reporting problems in F&O split so
cleanly into "the data is wrong" and "the layout is wrong".

Your half is the provider. In this lesson it fills `TmpItemSales`:

| Field         | Meaning       |
| ------------- | ------------- |
| `ItemGroupId` | Item group    |
| `ItemId`      | Item number   |
| `ItemName`    | Product name  |
| `SalesQty`    | Quantity      |
| `LineAmount`  | Net amount    |

<Aside title="What this is not">
  The **Report** tab applies the grouping and totals a design would apply. It is not SSRS:
  no page layout engine, no expression language, no subreports. What you are learning is the
  provider side, which is the X++ side, and the half that is actually yours.
</Aside>

</Step>

<Step id="fill-the-table">

## Your turn

Write a provider that copies every sales line into the report table.

For each `SalesLine`, join `InventTable` on `ItemId` so you can carry the group and the
name across, and insert one row with:

- `ItemGroupId`, `ItemId`, `ItemName` from the item
- `SalesQty` and `LineAmount` from the line

Then log `Provider wrote 6 rows`.

Open the **Report** tab afterwards — the design has grouped your rows by item group and
totalled the amounts, and you never told it to.

</Step>

<Step id="shape-is-yours">


The design groups and totals. That does not mean the provider should hand it everything and
shrug.

A provider that returns forty thousand rows so the design can total them into six lines has
moved the work to the wrong place: the rows cross a process boundary, get held in memory,
and get thrown away. A provider that aggregates first returns six rows.

The rule of thumb:

- **aggregate in the provider** when the report never shows the detail
- **return detail** when the reader can expand a group to see the lines

Both are correct reports. Only one of them is the right one for a given design, and it is
your call, not the design's.

</Step>

<Step id="aggregate-first">

## Your turn

Write the aggregating version.

Produce **one row per item** rather than one per sales line, with `SalesQty` and
`LineAmount` summed across that item's lines. Keep `ItemGroupId` and `ItemName` as before.

Six lines become five rows, because `F-100` is sold on two different orders.

Then log `Aggregated to 5 rows`.

There is more than one honest way to do this. A `while select` over the items that appear on
a line, with an inner loop summing that item's lines, is the readable one.

</Step>

<Step id="empty-report">


A provider that finds nothing writes nothing, and the design prints an empty report. That is
correct behaviour, and it is worth seeing on purpose — because in production it is the
symptom people misread most often.

Run the example. It filters on an item group that does not exist, writes nothing, and the
Report tab says so.

When a real report comes back empty, the question is almost never "is the design broken".
It is "what did the provider's query actually match" — and you answer that by reading the
SQL trace, which is right there in the next tab.

</Step>
